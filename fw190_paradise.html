Luftwaffe · Western Front · 1941–45

Focke-Wulf
Fw 190.

Kurt Tank's radial-engined masterpiece gave the Luftwaffe air superiority over the Channel Front from late 1941 and outclassed everything the RAF could put against it for the better part of a year. For scale modellers it means the most complex BMW 801 cowling in single-engine modelling, a huge variety of camouflage schemes and unit markings, and some of the finest injection kits ever produced.

Variants — from Würger to Dora

The Fw 190 progressed through more distinct production variants than almost any other WW2 fighter. Understanding which variant you're modelling determines the cowling shape, antenna layout, undercarriage legs, tail shape and armament configuration — all of which differ visibly between marks.

A-1 · First Production
Fw 190 A-1
1941 · Channel Front debut
The first production Fw 190, entering service with JG 26 at Le Bourget in August 1941. Equipped with four 7.92mm MG 17s only — the definitive armament wasn't yet fitted. Overheating problems with the early BMW 801 C-1 engine led to rapid development of the A-2. Rare subject but historically important as the aircraft that shocked RAF pilots expecting to meet something no better than the Bf 109F.
Channel Front
A-2 · First Major Version
Fw 190 A-2
1941–42 · Channel Front
Resolved the BMW 801 C overheating problem. Added two 20mm MG 151 cannon outboard of the undercarriage plus the original four MG 17s — six guns total. This was the version that dominated the Channel Front through the winter of 1941–42 and inflicted serious losses on RAF Fighter Command during the Circus operations. The most historical significant early Würger variant for BoB modellers.
Channel Front
A-3 · Production Refinement
Fw 190 A-3
1942 · Multi-front
Updated to the BMW 801 D-2 engine producing 1,700hp — a significant power increase. Retained the A-2 armament. First variant to see service on the Eastern Front in significant numbers alongside continued Channel Front operations. Also the first variant to be deployed to North Africa in small numbers. The A-3 is the foundation of the classic mid-war Würger and represents the best balance of the early A-series.
Multi-Front
A-4 · Most Produced Early
Fw 190 A-4
1942–43 · All fronts
The most produced early A-series variant. Added a new FuG 16Z radio with the distinctive whip antenna below the fuselage — a key identification feature. The A-4/U1 was a dual-control trainer; the A-4/U3 was a fighter-bomber with a centreline bomb rack. Many major Experten including Walter Nowotny scored heavily while flying A-4s on the Eastern Front. The antenna below the fuselage is present on the A-4 and all subsequent variants.
All Fronts
A-5 · Extended Cowling
Fw 190 A-5
1942–43 · All fronts
The engine mounting was moved forward by 15cm to improve handling — this is clearly visible as a slightly longer cowling ahead of the wing root compared to the A-4. The most important single external identification feature between early and mid A-series variants. The A-5 introduced the Umrüst-Bausätze (Rüstsätze) system of field modification kits, making it an extremely versatile platform. Hans-Joachim Marseille was one of the first to fly this variant.
Key Variant
A-6 · Heavy Fighter
Fw 190 A-6
1943 · Western Front bomber defence
A revised wing with four 20mm cannon replacing the mixed MG/cannon armament — all four outboard positions now carried MG 151/20. Built specifically for bomber interception as USAAF daylight raids intensified over Germany. The A-6's heavier firepower came at the cost of performance, but was necessary to bring down heavily armed B-17s and B-24s. Often fitted with the R6 modification adding two more 20mm cannon under the wings.
Bomber Destroyer
A-7 · Refined Armament
Fw 190 A-7
1943–44
The fuselage-mounted 7.92mm MG 17s were replaced by two 13mm MG 131s — visibly larger breeches that created a distinctive pair of bulges on the upper cowling immediately behind the spinner. These bulges are one of the most important identification features for the A-7 and A-8. The larger-calibre fuselage guns significantly improved firepower against bombers. Produced in relatively small numbers before being superseded by the A-8.
Cowling Bulges
A-8 · Definitive Würger
Fw 190 A-8
1944 · Most kitted variant
The definitive A-series variant and the most produced Fw 190 of all — over 6,500 built. BMW 801 D-2 engine, MG 131 cowling bulges, four 20mm wing cannon, increased fuel capacity. The standard Luftwaffe fighter of late 1944. Also the baseline for the A-8/R2 Sturmbock heavy interceptor with 30mm cannon and armour glass, and the A-8/R11 all-weather fighter. Eduard's ProfiPACK and Tamiya's long-running 1:48 kit both cover this variant.
Most Produced
D-9 · Long-Nose Dora
Fw 190 D-9 "Dora"
1944–45 · Superior high-altitude fighter
Completely redesigned front fuselage housing a liquid-cooled Junkers Jumo 213A engine instead of the BMW 801 radial — immediately identifiable by the longer, more tapered nose and the annular radiator around the engine. The D-9 restored performance parity with the P-51D and late Spitfires at altitude and was arguably the best piston-engined fighter in Luftwaffe service. The distinctive blue-grey engine cowling was standard on early production D-9s.
Jumo Engine
F-8 · Schlachtgeschwader
Fw 190 F-8
1943–45 · Ground attack
The dedicated ground attack variant — similar to the A-8 but with the outer 20mm cannon removed and replaced by a centreline bomb rack carrying a 500kg bomb or multiple smaller stores. Strengthened undercarriage and armour protection added. Served with Schlachtgeschwader (ground attack wings) on the Eastern Front and in Italy. The F-8 wore the same camouflage as the A-8 but markings were often more elaborate with unit insignia.
Ground Attack
Key identification guide

The three most visible differences between A-series variants: (1) A-1 to A-4 have a shorter engine cowling than A-5 onwards — look at the gap between spinner and wing root leading edge. (2) A-7 and A-8 have the distinctive paired MG 131 bulges on the upper cowling. (3) A-6 has a symmetrical four-cannon wing; earlier variants have the mixed MG/cannon layout visible as different gun barrel diameters in the wing leading edge.

Scale effect — lightening guide

Lighten RLM 74 and 75 by 10–15% for 1:48, 15–20% for 1:72. The eye reads smaller objects as darker than they are — lighter paint produces the correct visual impression at scale. RLM 76 should be lightened a further 5% over the upper surface adjustment. The difference between 74 and 75 should remain clearly visible — don't over-lighten 75 to the point where it blends into 76.

Reichsverteidigung markings

From late 1943, Defence of the Reich (Reichsverteidigung) units used a system of coloured fuselage bands and geometric symbols to identify which Geschwader an aircraft belonged to, enabling ground controllers to direct them. JG 1 used a single vertical white band; JG 2 used a double vertical; JG 3 used a horizontal band. These bands appear on the rear fuselage between the cockade and the tail. Most 1:48 kits include decals for at least one RVD marking scheme.

Experten — six pilot profiles

The Fw 190 produced some of the highest-scoring aces of the war. These six profiles cover the most historically significant and most modelled subjects across the main theatres.

Oberstleutnant Josef "Pips" Priller
JG 26 Schlageter · Channel Front
101 aerial victories · 11 four-engine bombers

Geschwaderkommodore of JG 26 for most of its wartime existence, Priller was the soul of the Channel Front Fw 190 operations. His personal aircraft "Jutta" — named after his wife — is one of the most documented and modelled Fw 190s of the war, a dark scheme with yellow spinner and personal markings. Priller and his wingman Heinz Wodarczyk were famously the only Luftwaffe pilots to attack the D-Day beaches, making a single low-level pass over Sword Beach on 6 June 1944.

Modelling reference Fw 190 A-7, "Jutta", JG 26, late 1943. Dark mottle scheme over standard RLM 74/75/76 base. Yellow spiral spinner. White "13" fuselage number. Eduard ProfiPACK 82145 includes this marking option.
Hauptmann Walter Nowotny
JG 54 Grünherz · Eastern Front
258 aerial victories · First pilot to 250

The first pilot in history to score 250 aerial victories, Nowotny achieved the majority of his score flying Fw 190s with JG 54 on the Eastern Front. He was awarded the Knight's Cross with Oak Leaves, Swords and Diamonds — Germany's highest decoration. His aircraft typically wore JG 54's distinctive green heart emblem. Later commanded JG Nowotny flying Me 262 jets, where he was killed in combat in November 1944 at the age of 23.

Modelling reference Fw 190 A-5, JG 54, Eastern Front 1943. RLM 74/75/76 with yellow Eastern Front cowling tip and fuselage band. Green heart emblem on cowling. White "1" fuselage number.
Major Egon Mayer
JG 2 Richthofen · Channel Front
102 aerial victories · 26 four-engine bombers

One of the most innovative tactical thinkers among Fw 190 pilots, Mayer developed the head-on attack against USAAF bomber formations — a technique later adopted across the Luftwaffe. The direct frontal approach exploited the B-17's relatively weak forward-firing armament. Mayer scored 26 four-engine bomber kills, a remarkable total against heavily defended aircraft. Killed in combat on 2 March 1944 during a B-17 interception — shot down by a P-47 Thunderbolt escort.

Modelling reference Fw 190 A-4/5, JG 2, Channel Front 1943. Standard RLM 74/75/76 scheme. Yellow spinner with black spiral. III. Gruppe white bar. Numerous decal sheets cover this scheme.
Leutnant Otto Kittel
JG 54 Grünherz · Eastern Front
267 aerial victories · Fourth highest of the war

The fourth highest-scoring ace of the entire war, Kittel achieved his extraordinary score almost entirely on the Eastern Front flying Fw 190s with JG 54. Unlike many high-scorers, Kittel operated primarily as a lone hunter rather than in planned tactical formations, exploiting his exceptional situational awareness. He was killed on 16 February 1945 during an Il-2 ground attack mission when his aircraft was hit by return fire. He was 23 years old with 267 confirmed kills.

Modelling reference Fw 190 A-6/A-8, JG 54, 1943–44. Heavy Eastern Front mottling over RLM 76 base. Yellow fuselage band. Green heart emblem. Kittel's personal aircraft is documented in multiple reference photographs.
Oberleutnant Hans Dortenmann
JG 54 / III. JG 3 · D-9 Dora
38 aerial victories · Dora specialist

One of the best-documented D-9 Dora pilots, Dortenmann flew with the elite III./JG 54 and later III./JG 3, defending Luftwaffe jet bases from Allied fighter-bombers in the closing months of the war. His aircraft "Black 1" is one of the most referenced Dora subjects for modellers — the distinctive two-tone scheme with the yellow-green rear band and the JG 54 green heart is featured in virtually every Fw 190 D-9 reference publication.

Modelling reference Fw 190 D-9 "Black 1", III./JG 3, 1944–45. RLM 76 lower fuselage, mottled upper surfaces. Yellow-green rear fuselage band. One of the most accurately documented D-9 subjects.
Major Anton Hackl
JG 11 · Defence of the Reich
192 aerial victories · Including B-17 specialists

One of the leading Defence of the Reich aces, Hackl flew A-8 and A-8/R2 Sturmbock variants against USAAF bomber formations over Germany. The Sturmbock was a heavily armed and armoured version specifically designed to break up bomber boxes — the additional armour around the cockpit and the 30mm BK 3 cannon made it visually distinctive and a fascinating modelling subject. Hackl survived the war despite numerous combats with heavily armed B-17s and their P-51 escorts.

Modelling reference Fw 190 A-8/R2 Sturmbock, JG 11, 1944. Reichsverteidigung horizontal band. Note the distinctive 30mm gun fairings under the nose. Eduard's ProfiPACK offers this variant.

Weathering by theatre

Fw 190 weathering varied dramatically by theatre and operational intensity. Channel Front aircraft were relatively well maintained; Eastern Front machines showed heavy wear from poor airfield conditions, extreme temperatures and high sortie rates.

Channel Front · France
JG 2, JG 26 · 1941–44
Exhaust staining
Moderate — black-brown streaking behind exhaust stacks on the upper cowling, lighter on the lower. BMW 801 exhausts produce distinctive patterns.
Paint wear
Well-maintained units — chipping primarily on walkways, leading edges and cowling fasteners. Not excessive by Eastern Front standards.
Mud / soil
Northern French chalk and clay soils — warm buff tones (Vallejo 70.916 Sand Yellow) on undercarriage legs and bays. Often washed off between sorties at established airfields.
Eastern Front · Russia
JG 51, JG 54 · 1942–45
Exhaust staining
Heavy — high sortie rates meant aircraft rarely cleaned. Dark brown-black streaks extending well back from exhaust stacks. Apply heavier than on Western Front subjects.
Paint wear
Extensive chipping and fading, particularly in summer months. RLM 76 undersurfaces often showing through on leading edges. Yellow markings sun-bleached to near-cream.
Mud / soil
Dark Russian earth tones — Vallejo 70.984 Flat Brown with Vallejo 70.950 Black added. Thick accumulations on undercarriage legs and lower fuselage sides in wet seasons.
Winter distemper
Chalk-based whitewash applied over standard scheme, wearing rapidly in service. Represent with thin Vallejo 70.951 White applied with a wide brush — allow base scheme to show through.
Defence of the Reich · Germany
JG 1, JG 11 · 1943–45
Exhaust staining
Moderate to heavy — aircraft flew multiple interception sorties per day during major raids. Streaking behind exhaust stacks prominent.
Paint condition
Mixed — some aircraft were freshly painted, others showed extensive battle damage repairs with mismatched patches. Replacement panels in slightly different batch colours are historically accurate and add visual interest.
Gun blast staining
Heavier than on Western Front Fw 190s due to more firing passes against bombers. Dark grey-black staining radiating from all gun ports. A-8/R2 aircraft show additional blast staining around the 30mm gun port.
D-9 Dora · Late War
JG 54, JG 3 · 1944–45
Cowling tone
The blue-grey Jumo cowling on early D-9s is a distinctive colour. It often shows oil streaking downward from the cowling access panels — dark brown-black streaks on the blue-grey surface.
Rear fuselage band
The yellow-green band fades significantly and often shows brush marks from field application. Represent as a semi-transparent coat rather than solid colour — let the RLM 76 show through slightly.
Overall condition
Late-war aircraft were often hurriedly produced and showed rapid wear. Factory-fresh appearance unlikely — even relatively new aircraft show significant surface wear in period photographs.

BMW 801 exhaust — specific technique

The BMW 801 radial engine produced heavy exhaust staining in a distinctive pattern unique to radial-engined aircraft. Unlike inline engines, the staining fans out from multiple exhaust stacks arranged around the cowling, creating a characteristic star-burst pattern of dark streaks radiating backward from the cowling ring.

Apply the staining in two stages: first a dark brown-black (Tamiya XF-64 Red Brown + XF-1 Black, 70:30) for the primary staining, then a lighter grey-black post-shade over the primary coat to add depth. The streaks should extend back 20–30mm on a 1:48 model, thinning and lightening as they extend rearward. The lower cowling shows less staining than the upper — oil from the engine tends to deposit on the lower cowling surfaces separately.
