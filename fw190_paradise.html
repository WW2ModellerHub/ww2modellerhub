Production variants — Würger to Dora
The Fw 190 progressed through more distinct production variants than almost any other WW2 fighter. Understanding the differences between marks determines the cowling shape, antenna layout, wing profile and armament — all clearly visible at scale. The three most reliable external identification features are the nose length (A-5 onwards is longer), the MG 131 cowling bulges (A-7 and A-8 only), and the wing oil cooler blister (Emil-era, absent on all Fw 190 variants).

Long-nose Dora Jumo engine
Completely redesigned nose with Jumo 213A liquid-cooled engine replacing the BMW 801 radial. The longer, tapered nose and annular radiator make it immediately identifiable. Restored performance parity with P-51D and late Spitfires at altitude. The blue-grey Jumo cowling and yellow-green rear fuselage band are characteristic of early D-9s.
Key identification guide
Three reliable differences: (1) A-5 onwards has a noticeably longer cowling ahead of the wing root than the A-1 to A-4. (2) A-7 and A-8 have the distinctive paired MG 131 bulges on the upper cowling behind the spinner — absent on all earlier variants. (3) The D-9 is conclusively identified by its long tapered nose, the annular radiator ring and the yellow-green rear fuselage band on most examples.
RLM paint codes — four brands
The Fw 190 wore RLM 74/75/76 throughout its operational life. All codes below are cross-referenced across Humbrol, Tamiya Acrylic, Vallejo Model Color and AK Interactive. Lighten upper surface colours by 10–15% for 1:48, 15–20% for 1:72 to compensate for scale effect.

Upper surfaces · Grey-Green. One of two upper surface colours used together in an irregular pattern. Cooler and more neutral than RLM 71 it replaced. Primary mottling colour on Gustav fuselage sides.

Jagdgeschwader — unit markings reference
Eight major Jagdgeschwader flew the Fw 190 in combat. Each unit developed its own distinctive cowling colours, fuselage markings and individual aircraft identifiers. The Channel Front units — particularly JG 26 — are the most extensively photographed and documented Fw 190 subjects for modellers.
JG 2
JG 2 Richthofen
Channel Front · 1941–44
Channel Front Yellow spiral spinner
Yellow spiral spinner on many aircraft. Individual Experten markings. "R" Gruppe letter behind cockade. Fuselage white bars for III. Gruppe. Egon Mayer (102 kills, 26 four-engine bombers — developed the head-on attack technique), Erich Leie and Joachim Müncheberg all flew here. One of the two primary Channel Front Geschwader.
Distinctive marking
Yellow/black spiral spinner · "R" fuselage letter · White Gruppe bars
JG 26
JG 26 Schlageter
Channel Front · 1941–45
Channel Front Most documented
The most documented Western Front Fw 190 unit — extensive photo record from occupied France. Josef "Pips" Priller (101 kills, "Jutta" personal aircraft) was Kommodore for most of the war. Black/white/yellow diagonal stripes on many aircraft. Individual Staffel colours on cowling rings. Priller and Wodarczyk's famous lone D-Day beach strafing pass.
Distinctive marking
Diagonal stripe patterns · Individual Staffel cowling rings · Yellow spinners
JG 54
JG 54 Grünherz
Eastern Front · 1942–45
Eastern Front Green heart badge
Green heart Geschwaderwappen on cowling — the most recognisable Eastern Front Fw 190 badge. Yellow Eastern Front identification markings. Nowotny (258 kills — first to 250), Kittel (267 kills — fourth highest ever) and Philipp all flew here. Heavy mottling common. Leningrad sector operations.
Distinctive marking
Green heart on cowling · Yellow Eastern Front fuselage band · Heavy mottling
JG 51
JG 51 Mölders
Eastern Front · 1942–45
Eastern Front Mottled schemes
Yellow Eastern Front fuselage bands and cowling tips. Red band behind cockade for II. Gruppe. Often heavily mottle-camouflaged with RLM 74/75 applied by brush over RLM 76 base — producing the most irregular and complex camouflage of any Fw 190 unit. Friedrich-Karl Müller and Günther Schack flew here.
Distinctive marking
Yellow Eastern Front ID · Red II. Gruppe band · Heavy brush-applied mottling
JG 1
JG 1
Defence of the Reich · 1943–44
Defence of Reich Vertical white band
JG 1 used the vertical white fuselage band Reichsverteidigung identification marking. Often A-6/R6 aircraft with underwing 20mm pods visible. Anti-bomber specialists defending against increasing USAAF daylight raids. Home Defence white band applied to rear fuselage.
Distinctive marking
Single vertical white fuselage band · A-6/R6 underwing cannon pods
JG 11
JG 11
Northern Germany · 1943–45
Defence of Reich Horizontal band
Horizontal bar Reichsverteidigung marking. A-8/R2 Sturmbock variants common — the 30mm BK 3 fairings under the nose and additional cockpit armour plate create a distinctive stepped cowling profile. Anton Hackl (192 kills) flew here. Hugo Frey was the unit's leading Experten.
Distinctive marking
Horizontal white band · A-8/R2 Sturmbock 30mm nose fairings
JG 3
III./JG 3 Udet

D-9 Doras Jet base defence
Dora variants defending Luftwaffe jet airfields from P-47 and P-51 attacks. The famous "Black 1" of Dortenmann is one of the most referenced D-9 subjects — JG 54 green heart on the cowling, yellow-green rear band, dark upper mottling. Some of the most photographed late-war Fw 190 subjects.

JG 52
JG 52
Eastern Front · 1942–44
Eastern Front Mixed Fw/Bf ops
Standard Eastern Front yellow markings. Some aircraft with extensive white winter distemper. Primarily a Bf 109 unit but operated Fw 190s in several Gruppen from 1943. Gerhard Barkhorn (301 kills — second highest ever) and Hermann Graf served here. Complex mix of individual aircraft markings.
Distinctive marking
Yellow Eastern Front ID · Winter whitewash common · Individual Experten markings
Reichsverteidigung band colours
From late 1943, Defence of the Reich units used coloured fuselage bands for ground control identification. JG 1: single vertical white band. JG 2: double vertical white band. JG 3: horizontal white band. JG 11: horizontal white band with different positioning. These bands appear on the rear fuselage between the cockade and the fin root. Most Eduard ProfiPACK kits include decals for at least one RVD option.

Weathering by theatre and variant
Fw 190 weathering varied dramatically by theatre and operational intensity. Channel Front aircraft at established bases were relatively well maintained; Eastern Front machines showed heavy wear from poor airfield surfaces, extreme temperature variation and high sortie rates. The D-9 Dora has its own distinctive weathering characteristics around the Jumo cowling.
W. Fr
Channel Front
JG 2, JG 26 · France · 1941–44
Northern France Well maintained
Exhaust: Moderate — black-brown streaking behind stacks on upper cowling, lighter below.

Paint wear: Moderate. Chipping primarily on walkways, leading edges and cowling fasteners. Well-maintained units — not as extreme as Eastern Front.

Soil: Northern French chalk and clay — warm buff tones (Vallejo 70.916 Sand Yellow) on undercarriage. Often cleaned between sorties at established airfields.
E. Fr
Eastern Front
JG 51, JG 54 · Russia · 1942–45
Eastern Front Heavy wear
Exhaust: Heavy — high sortie rates, rarely cleaned. Dark brown-black streaks extending well back. Apply heavier than on any Western Front subject.

Paint wear: Extensive chipping, fading. Yellow Eastern Front markings often sun-bleached to near-cream after several months.

Soil: Dark Russian earth — Vallejo 70.984 Flat Brown + 70.950 Black. Thick accumulations on undercarriage in spring/autumn mud seasons.

Winter: Chalk whitewash applied over standard scheme. Thin Vallejo 70.951 White, brush-applied, showing base scheme through worn areas.
RVD
Defence of the Reich
JG 1, JG 11 · Germany · 1943–45
High sortie rate Battle damage
Exhaust: Moderate to heavy — multiple interception sorties per day during major raids.

Paint condition: Mixed — some aircraft freshly painted, others with extensive battle damage repairs. Mismatched replacement panels in slightly different batch colours are historically accurate.

Gun blast: Heavier than Channel Front — more firing passes against bombers. Dark grey-black staining from all gun ports. A-8/R2 aircraft show additional blast staining around 30mm cannon port.
D-9
Dora — late war
JG 54, JG 3 · 1944–45
D-9 specific Jumo cowling
Jumo cowling: The blue-grey cowling shows oil streaking downward from cowling access panels — dark brown-black streaks on the blue-grey surface.

Rear band: Yellow-green band fades and shows brush marks from field application. Apply as semi-transparent coat — let the RLM 76 show through.

Overall: Late-war aircraft were hurriedly produced and showed rapid wear. Even relatively new aircraft show significant surface weathering in period photographs.
BMW 801 exhaust technique
The radial engine produces exhaust staining in a distinctive star-burst pattern — multiple stacks radiating around the cowling ring, each leaving dark streaks extending backward. Apply in two stages: dark brown-black (Tamiya XF-64 + XF-1, 70:30) for the primary staining, then lighter grey-black post-shade for depth. Streaks should extend 20–30mm on a 1:48 model, thinning and lightening rearward. Lower cowling shows less staining than upper — apply accordingly.
