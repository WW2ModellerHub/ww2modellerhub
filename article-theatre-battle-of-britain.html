Battle of Britain Route

A modelling route through the summer of 1940: RAF Fighter Command, Luftwaffe raids, short-range combat, patched aircraft and early-war camouflage decisions.

The story

The Battle of Britain route is about urgency. Aircraft were scrambled, landed, repaired, rearmed and sent back up again, often from grass airfields under direct threat. Hurricanes carried much of the bomber-fighting burden, Spitfires became the visual icon, and Bf 109s operated at the edge of their useful range. The result for a modeller is not a parade-ground finish, but a machine that looks operational, recently maintained and under pressure.

Aircraft to build

Start with a Hurricane Mk I or Spitfire Mk I/II if you want the RAF story. Add a Bf 109E, Bf 110 or Do 17 if you want the Luftwaffe side of the route. The best subjects are aircraft with squadron codes, visible service wear and clear period markings rather than generic later-war schemes.

Paint logic

RAF day fighters sit around Dark Earth and Dark Green over Sky or earlier underside variations. Luftwaffe subjects lean into RLM 65, 02 and 71, with yellow tactical markings appearing as the campaign develops. This is a theatre where getting the date right matters: early 1940, peak summer and late campaign aircraft do not all look the same.

Weathering logic

Keep it restrained but purposeful. Use exhaust staining, light wing-root wear, gun staining, dusty grass-field undersides, paint touch-ups and slightly mismatched panels. Avoid heavy late-war chipping unless the reference photo supports it. The story is operational tempo, not dereliction.

Good modelling route

Build a Hurricane first, then a Spitfire, then a Bf 109E. That gives the clearest visual comparison between RAF and Luftwaffe design, colours and wear patterns. Pair this route with the paint codes page and the aircraft comparison page.
