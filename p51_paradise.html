USAAF long-range escort campaign · 1944–1945

The P-51 Mustang was the long-range escort fighter that helped the USAAF seize daylight air superiority over Europe and later flew major Pacific VLR missions from Iwo Jima. Operating mainly with the 8th Air Force from East Anglian bases, Mustang groups escorted bombers to Berlin and beyond, then ranged ahead to attack enemy fighters, airfields and transport. This page focuses on the most model-friendly fighter groups, key pilots, unit colours and markings. Click any card to see pilots, markings, bases and decal-ready subject ideas.

Choosing your Mustang kit

The kit choice now supports Europe, Mediterranean, Pacific VLR and CBI subjects. Use P-51D/K kits for Iwo Jima, Ie Shima/Okinawa and late-war 8th AF subjects; use P-51B/C kits for early ETO or CBI-style Olive Drab builds. Choose by variant, theatre, tanks/stores and decal sheet.

Aftermarket for the P-51 Mustang

Aftermarket is now split by purpose: decals and markings, Pacific/VLR loadout, cockpit, exterior detail and masks. For Pacific subjects, drop tanks, sun-faded weathering and correct unit markings are as important as cockpit detail.

USAAF Mustang paint colours

Mustang colour treatment is dominated by two main looks: early-war Olive Drab over Neutral Gray on P-51B/C aircraft, and the classic late-war natural-metal finish on P-51D/K aircraft. Group colours, anti-glare panels, invasion stripes and tail markings do most of the visual work on a finished model.

Roundel & marking notes

National insignia: Standard USAAF star-and-bar markings carried on wings and fuselage, with blue outline and red-bar period changes depending on date.

Group colours: Nose colours, tail markings, checker patterns and squadron codes vary sharply by fighter group — one of the reasons the Mustang is such a strong decal subject.

Common extras: Olive Drab anti-glare panel, black serials, invasion stripes in mid-1944, and highly visible unit colours on spinner, nose or tail depending on subject.

Exhausts & Nose
The Packard Merlin nose, anti-glare panel and exhaust staining are major P-51 visual cues. Natural-metal subjects show exhaust staining clearly, usually sweeping back as a brown-grey stain over metal and the painted anti-glare panel.

Airframe & Natural Metal
Natural-metal Mustangs need panel variation, not a single silver finish. Keep the laminar-flow wing smoother than the fuselage, study the radiator scoop and avoid over-emphasising every panel line equally.
