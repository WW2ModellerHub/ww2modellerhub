Base Coating
Base Coating
Airbrushing the BoB Scheme — Dark Earth, Dark Green & Sky
Intermediate

The standard 1940 RAF camouflage uses two uppers (Dark Earth + Dark Green) in an asymmetric soft-edge pattern over Sky undersides. Getting clean demarcation without masking is the key skill.

  1. Prime with grey primer, check for sink marks and fill with putty
  2. Apply Sky (Humbrol 90 / Tamiya XF-21) to all undersides first — it's the lightest colour
  3. Thin Dark Earth to milk consistency (PSI 15–18), apply freehand with slow elliptical sweeps
  4. Apply Dark Green second — the soft edge between the two uppers is airbrushed freehand at distance 8–10cm
  5. Seal with gloss varnish before any decals or panel washes
Tip — Real demarcation lines were painted by hand with a brush in the factory. They're softer than masking tape gives you. Work 10–12cm away with reduced pressure for the boundary passes.
Recommended paints: Humbrol 29 (Dark Earth), Humbrol 30 (Dark Green), Humbrol 90 (Sky) · Tamiya XF-49, XF-61, XF-21
Base Coating
Cockpit Interior — Brush-Painting Interior Grey-Green
Beginner

RAF cockpits in 1940 were painted in a mixed Interior Grey-Green — a complex non-standard colour that varied by manufacturer. The closest modern matches and how to apply them by brush.

  1. Mix Humbrol 78 (Cockpit Green) with a touch of Humbrol 75 (Bronze Green) — 3:1 ratio
  2. Apply two thin coats to all cockpit parts before assembly — allow to fully dry between coats
  3. Drybrush a lighter mix (add 10% white) over raised detail to pick up instrument bezels and levers
  4. Wash with dark brown oil diluted in white spirit to deepen recesses
  5. Paint instrument faces Flat Black, then add tiny dot of gloss for instrument glass
Tip — Vallejo 71.323 is the best single-bottle match for Supermarine Interior Grey-Green. Hawker's was slightly darker.
Recommended paints: Humbrol 78 · Vallejo 71.323 (Interior Green) · AK RC257
Pre-Shading
Pre-Shading
Black Panel Line Pre-shade
Intermediate

Spraying a dark pre-shade over panel lines before the main colour adds depth and tonal variation that breaks up the uniform look of a fresh topcoat. Subtle is the key word — over-done pre-shading looks artificial.

  1. Prime as normal. Let cure fully (24 hours for lacquer primer)
  2. Reduce Flat Black 1:3 with thinner — much thinner than normal airbrushing
  3. Drop PSI to 10–12 and reduce needle opening. Spray hairline passes directly along panel lines
  4. Apply topcoat semi-transparently — thin 1:2 — in multiple passes. The pre-shade bleeds through subtly
  5. If too visible, add another pass of topcoat. Always easier to add than to remove
Tip — For RAF Dark Earth and Dark Green, pre-shade in dark brown rather than black. Pure black through warm earth tones reads as grey and looks wrong.
Products used: Tamiya XF-1 Flat Black · AK Real Colors RC-239 (Dark Brown pre-shade)

Pre-Shading
Colour Modulation — Lighter Panel Centres
Advanced

Real aircraft fade unevenly — panels exposed to more sun bleach faster. Modulation mimics this by painting each panel slightly lighter at its centre, making the model look like a real aircraft rather than a plastic toy.

  1. Apply base colour normally
  2. Mix base colour + 15–20% white for the highlight mix
  3. At PSI 8–10, spray tight ellipses at the centre of major panels, avoiding the edges
  4. Work panel by panel — fuselage, then wings, then control surfaces
  5. Unify with a very thin filter (oil paint diluted heavily in white spirit) to blend transitions
Tip — Look at period photographs of Hurricanes on grass airfields in summer. The upper wing root panels are almost always bleached to near-khaki. The wingtips stay darker, protected by shadow.
Panel Line Washes
Washes
Dark Brown Enamel Panel Wash — RAF Uppers
Beginner

A dark brown wash is ideal for the earth-toned RAF upper scheme — black reads too stark. The enamel formula allows clean removal with white spirit and cotton buds for sharp results.

  1. Apply a gloss varnish coat first — washes flow better on gloss and are easier to clean up
  2. Mix dark brown oil paint (Raw Umber) in white spirit 1:8 — water-consistency
  3. Load a fine brush and touch the panel lines — capillary action does the work, do not scrub
  4. Let sit 15–20 min until tacky. Use cotton buds dampened with white spirit to wipe flat surfaces
  5. Always wipe in one direction (forward, as if the aircraft is moving) — this creates realistic streaking
Tip — Flory Models Dark Dirt wash works straight from the bottle on Dark Earth panels and requires no mixing. Ideal for beginners.
Products: Flory Models Dark Dirt · AK Panel Line Wash Dark Brown (AK045) · W&N Burnt Umber oil

Washes
Blue-Grey Wash for Sky Undersides
Intermediate

Sky-coloured undersides need a cooler wash — dark brown reads wrong on a pale blue-grey. A thin mix of Payne's Grey oil gives the right cool shadow without looking dirty.

  1. Gloss varnish the undersides before washing
  2. Mix Payne's Grey oil paint in white spirit 1:10 — very thin
  3. Flow into all panel lines and rivet rows on the underside
  4. Clean up excess with white spirit on a flat brush, wiping forward
  5. Pay attention to the wheel bay edges — these collect more grime and can be darker
Tip — AK Interactive Light Grey Panel Line Wash (AK068) is a ready-to-use alternative that works well on Sky colours.
Products: W&N Payne's Grey oil · AK068 Light Grey wash · White spirit
Chipping & Wear
Chipping
Hairspray Technique — Realistic Paint Chipping
Advanced

The hairspray technique creates chips and scratches that reveal the aluminium airframe beneath. Ideal for heavily used BoB aircraft that would have been patched, repainted and worn through months of operation.

  1. Apply base in silver/aluminium — this represents the bare metal underneath
  2. Coat with ordinary cheap hairspray (water soluble is key) — 2–3 light passes
  3. Apply camouflage colours as normal over the hairspray layer
  4. Once fully dry, use a stiff damp brush or cocktail stick to scrape chips from high-wear areas
  5. Focus on wing leading edges, walkway areas, canopy rails and panel fasteners
Tip — BoB aircraft were repaired frequently. Patches of new paint over worn patches are authentic. Vary the chip size: tiny scratches on flat surfaces, larger chips on edges.
Products: Cheap supermarket hairspray · Tamiya X-11 Chrome Silver underneath · Stiff hog-hair brush

Chipping
Pencil Graphite — Metal Shine on Worn Edges
Beginner

The easiest way to add metallic sheen to worn edges. A soft graphite pencil rubbed along the leading edges and wing-walk areas mimics polished bare metal exposed by paint wear.

  1. Complete all painting and apply a semi-matte varnish
  2. Use a 4B–6B soft graphite pencil (Prismacolor Silver is even better)
  3. Rub lightly along the leading edges of wings, tailplane and fin
  4. Apply to the cockpit rail where pilots' gloves would wear the paint
  5. Blend with a fingertip for a subtle shine effect
Tip — Prismacolor Silver pencils give a more metallic finish than graphite. Available from art shops. One pencil does dozens of models.
Oil Paint Techniques
Oil Paints
Oil Streaking — Exhaust Staining & Rain Marks
Intermediate

Merlin engines produced heavy exhaust residue that stained the upper wing and fuselage surfaces aft of the exhausts. Rain and humidity created vertical streaks below panel joints. Both effects use thinned oil paints.

  1. Apply a matte or semi-matte varnish — streaks need tooth to grip
  2. For exhaust staining: mix Raw Umber + Lamp Black in white spirit and stipple aft of the exhausts
  3. Streak downward and backward with a flat brush dampened in clean white spirit
  4. For rain marks: thin Payne's Grey to near-invisible and draw thin vertical lines from panel joints
  5. Blend immediately with a dry brush — streaks should whisper, not shout
Tip — Reference period photographs of grounded Hurricanes before applying streaks. The staining pattern from the Merlin is very consistent and very distinctive.
Products: W&N Raw Umber oil · W&N Lamp Black · White spirit · AK Streaking Grime (AK012)

Oil Paints
Dot Filtering — Colour Variation Across Panels
Advanced

Dot filtering adds subtle tonal variation that gives large surfaces life and complexity. Small dots of different oil colours are applied randomly then blended to create a look of uneven wear and fading.

  1. Apply gloss varnish — this is essential for blending
  2. Place tiny dots of 3–4 oil colours across panels: Raw Umber, Yellow Ochre, Burnt Sienna, White
  3. Wait 10 minutes for the oil to begin to tack slightly
  4. Use a flat brush moistened with white spirit to blend the dots in one direction
  5. Work panel by panel. Allow 24h to cure before any further work
Tip — Keep dots small — 1–2mm. The instinct is to use too much oil. Three tiny dots per panel are enough on 1:48 scale. More reads as muddy.
Pigments & Pastels
Pigments
Mud & Dust — Undercarriage and Airfield Grime
Beginner

BoB airfields were grass fields — muddy in wet weather, dusty in summer. The undercarriage legs, wheel bays and lower fuselage of operational Hurricanes and Spitfires would have been caked in earth. Pigments are the quickest way to reproduce this.

  1. Apply a final matte varnish to the undercarriage area
  2. Brush dry pigment powder onto the undercarriage legs and wheel hubs with a flat brush
  3. Build up in layers — light earth first, then a touch of dark brown in the recesses
  4. Fix by misting isopropyl alcohol from 30cm — do not brush after fixing
  5. For wet mud effect: mix pigment with a tiny amount of PVA and stipple on
Tip — MIG Productions Earth pigment set includes the three tones you need for British summer grass airfields: light sand, medium earth and dark umber.
Products: MIG P025 (Light Dust) · MIG P028 (Dried Mud) · Isopropyl alcohol fixative

Decal Application
Decals
Applying RAF Roundels & Code Letters — Flat Surface
Beginner

Large roundels and code letters need careful handling. The decal film must be softened to conform to the panel surface without silvering. Gloss surface + setting solutions = invisible decals.

  1. Always apply decals over a gloss surface — matte surfaces trap air and cause silvering
  2. Apply Micro Set to the model surface — this is the adhesion step
  3. Float decal off backing paper, slide into position, absorb excess water with tissue
  4. Apply Micro Sol over the placed decal — this softens and snuggles it into surface detail
  5. Once dry (12h), seal with another gloss coat before any weathering
Tip — Aftermarket decals (Xtradecal, Techmod) are significantly thinner than kit decals. For 1940 Hurricanes and Spitfires, Xtradecal X48143 covers six BoB aircraft in 1:48 with authentic roundel types.
Products: Micro Set · Micro Sol · Xtradecal X48143 · Johnson's Klear for gloss coat
Varnish & Final Finish
Varnish
Final Matte Coat — Getting the Right Sheen for 1940 RAF
Beginner

1940 RAF aircraft were painted with a camouflage finish that was not completely flat — it had a slight eggshell sheen. Dead flat looks wrong and dusty. The right varnish makes the difference between a toy and a convincing model.

  1. Apply a gloss varnish over the completed weathering — this unifies everything
  2. For final coat: mix Tamiya X-21 Flat Base into X-22 Clear Gloss — 2:8 ratio
  3. This gives a satin/eggshell finish that matches the original RAF cellulose paint sheen
  4. Apply in thin coats at low PSI — avoid going too thick which creates a milky haze
  5. Canopy frames get a slightly glossier coat — polished perspex reflected light
Tip — Vallejo Satin Varnish airbrushed straight from the bottle gives an excellent ready-made eggshell finish. One coat is usually enough.
Products: Tamiya X-21 Flat Base + X-22 Clear · Vallejo Satin Varnish (702.522) · Mr Color GX114
