Battle of Britain · 10 July – 31 October 1940

The Hawker Hurricane carried the main weight of Fighter Command in the Battle of Britain. Hurricane squadrons attacked bomber formations, defended sector stations and absorbed heavy losses while remaining easier to repair in dispersed conditions. Choosing your Hurricane kit

For a Battle of Britain Hurricane Mk.I, check whether the subject needs fabric wings, metal wings, two-blade or three-blade propeller, early pole mast or later aerial fit.

Aftermarket for the Hurricane Mk.I

The Airfix cockpit benefits greatly from Eduard SPACE 3D. Master Model AM-48-108 brass Browning barrels transform the wing. Kabuki canopy masks are essential — the Hurricane's many curved panes are very hard to mask freehand.

Battle of Britain paint colours

Every BoB Hurricane wore Dark Earth and Dark Green upper surfaces, Sky (Type S) undersides and spinner. The split line on the Hurricane's curved fuselage creates a characteristic wavy demarcation — the A-scheme has brown on the starboard nose side.

Roundel & marking notes

July–Aug 1940: Type A1 roundels (no yellow ring) on fuselage. Type A upper wings. Type B lower wings. Large fin flash.

Sep–Oct 1940: Yellow outer ring on fuselage roundels increasingly common by October.

Code letters: Sky BS381C-216. ~24 inch height. Serial in black rear fuselage port side only. Some Hurricanes had a Sky spinner with a narrow black spiral stripe.

Stage-by-stage build guide for the Hurricane. Assumes 1:48, e.g. Airfix AX05126A.
Period-accurate weathering by base. All Hurricanes showed exhaust staining port upper cowling. Eight gun port blast marks along each leading edge. Port wing root walkway chipping is distinctive.
