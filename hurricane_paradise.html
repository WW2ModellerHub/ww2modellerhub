Choosing your Hurricane kit

Nine injection-moulded Hurricane kits are currently available across three scales. For a Battle of Britain build in 1:48, the Airfix A05127A is the recommendation for most builders — excellent accuracy, great fit, and outstanding value. If you want the finest Hurricane kit ever made, the Arma Hobby AH40015 Expert Set is extraordinary. In 1:72, the Arma Hobby 70019 / AH70024 is in a different league. Aftermarket for the Hurricane

The Airfix kit cockpit benefits hugely from the CMK CMK4352 resin set — it's the single best upgrade for the money. For gun barrels, the Master Model AM-48-172 brass set makes an immediate visible difference. Eduard's pre-cut canopy masks save time and improve results over freehand. Photo-etch is optional on the Airfix kit but nearly mandatory on the older Hasegawa and Eduard plastics. Battle of Britain paint colours

Every Hurricane in the Battle of Britain wore the same three-colour MAP scheme: Dark Earth and Dark Green on the upper surfaces in a disruptive splinter pattern, and Sky (Type S) on the undersides, spinner and fuselage band. The table below gives matched codes across all four major paint brands. Humbrol 29/30/90 are the classic references; Tamiya XF-52/XF-61/XF-21 are good for airbrush users; Vallejo offers superior coverage for brush painters. The cockpit, wheel bays and gear legs were Interior Green (Humbrol 78).

Roundel & marking notes

July–Aug 1940: Type A1 roundels (red/white/blue, no yellow ring) on fuselage. Type A upper wings. Type B lower wings (red/blue only). Large fin flash.

Sep–Oct 1940: Yellow outer ring on fuselage roundels increasingly common. Yellow border on upper wing roundels appearing late in period.

Code letters: Sky BS381C-216. Approx 24 inch height. Serial in black on rear fuselage port side only.

Cockpit
Modeller's note Interior is Titanine Interior Grey-Green — slightly warmer and greener than Spitfire. Instrument panel Flat Black. The ejector exhaust handles on the right side of the cockpit are a distinctive detail. Floor is darker and more worn than side walls. R4118 (Hurricane Heritage) has the most accessible authentic cockpit of any surviving example.

Exhaust Stacks & Staining
Modeller's note The Hurricane uses ejector exhaust stacks — one long casting per side, not individual stubs. The staining pattern is a single wide fan aft of the exhaust outlet, extending back to roughly the cockpit position. The stacks oxidise to a rusty brown-orange. Eduard Brassin 648580 gives you resin stacks with excellent casting detail for 1:48.

Undercarriage
Modeller's note Undercarriage legs are Interior Grey-Green — not camouflage colour. Wheel bay interior also grey-green. Mechanical parts on legs are bare aluminium. Tyres are warm mid-grey, not cold grey or black. The wheel bays are open when gear is down — they were frequently mud-spattered on BoB grass airfields.

Roundels, Codes & Markings
Modeller's note Roundel types changed during the Battle. Before ~Aug 1940: Type A (red/white/blue/yellow outer) on upper wings, Type A1 (no yellow) on fuselage, Type B (red/blue only) on lower wings. Code letters were Night Black 8" — not white. Fin flash was red/white/blue equal thirds. Spinners matched Sky colour. See the Reference tab for exact roundel dimensions by scale.

Airframe — Fabric Fuselage & Panel Detail
Modeller's note The Hurricane's rear fuselage is fabric over a tubular steel frame — not metal. The ribs show through the doped fabric as subtle raised lines, much more muted than metal panel lines. Do not scribe heavy panel lines on the rear fuselage. The transition from metal (forward of cockpit) to fabric occurs approximately at the cockpit rear.
