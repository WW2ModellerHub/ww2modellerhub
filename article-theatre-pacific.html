Pacific Route

Carrier decks, coral dust, salt air, humidity and naval weathering: the Pacific route is one of the most visually rewarding for WW2 aircraft modellers.

Pacific Route

The Pacific route is about environment. Aircraft lived on carriers, island strips and humid forward bases. Japanese finishes can show dramatic wear, while US Navy aircraft often show fading, staining and deck-handling marks. The theatre works best when you compare opponents side by side.

Period1941–45
Theatre lookIJN/IJAAF greens, US Navy blues
Best first buildZero or Hellcat
Weathering levelFaded, salty and chipped

Campaign timeline

1941–42

Early Japanese carrier power and lighter Zero finishes set the opening look.

1943

US Navy tri-colour schemes and island operations create richer weathering options.

1944–45

Gloss Sea Blue, carrier intensity and late-war Japanese wear change the visual story again.

Paint logic

Do not mix IJN and IJAAF colours casually; decide aircraft, service and date first.
US Navy routes move from blue-grey to tri-colour to Gloss Sea Blue.
Japanese greens, greys, primer behaviour and field wear need subject-specific reference.

Weathering recipe

Salt fading and sun bleaching on upper surfaces.
Wing-walk scuffs, deck handling marks and chipped high-traffic areas.
Coral dust around wheels and lower surfaces for island subjects.
Exhaust staining can be prominent, especially on hard-used naval aircraft.

Display idea

A simple carrier deck section or coral-strip base transforms the model. Add tie-down points, deck staining, oil marks or pale coral dust to explain the aircraft before the viewer reads the caption.
