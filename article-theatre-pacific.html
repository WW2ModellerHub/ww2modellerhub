Pacific Route

A theatre route for carrier decks, coral dust, island bases, salt air, IJN/IJAAF finishes and US Navy weathering logic.

The story

The Pacific route is about distance, heat, salt and naval operations. Aircraft flew from carriers, island strips and humid forward bases. Japanese aircraft often show dramatic paint wear in popular modelling subjects, while US Navy aircraft develop fading, exhaust staining, deck-handling scuffs and salt exposure. This theatre gives very strong visual variety.

Aircraft to build

Start with a Zero, Hellcat, Corsair or Dauntless. Add an Avenger, Kate, Val or Betty if you want the carrier and maritime strike story. The theatre is especially good for comparing Japanese carrier aircraft with US Navy subjects as the war progresses.

Paint logic

Japanese aircraft need careful handling: IJN and IJAAF colours are not interchangeable. Early Zero greys, later greens, primer behaviour and field wear all need subject-specific thinking. US Navy schemes move through blue-grey, tri-colour and Gloss Sea Blue routes. The paint story changes dramatically between 1941, 1943 and 1945.

Weathering logic

Use salt fading, exhaust staining, wing-walk wear, chipped high-traffic areas, oil streaks and coral dust. Carrier aircraft can show scuffed walkways and deck handling marks, while island-based aircraft may collect dust and sun fading. Avoid making every Japanese aircraft look stripped to bare metal unless the subject supports it.

Good modelling route

Build a Zero and Hellcat as a pair, then add a Corsair or Dauntless. That gives a strong route through early-war Japanese design, US Navy development and different weathering patterns.
