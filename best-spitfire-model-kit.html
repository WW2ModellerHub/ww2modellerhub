Spitfire kit buyer’s guide

Best Spitfire Model Kit

The Spitfire is the most kitted British aircraft ever made, so the real question is not “which kit?” but “which mark, which scale and how much work do you want?”. This guide narrows it down.

Start with the mark, not the box

There is no single best Spitfire kit because there is no single Spitfire. A Battle of Britain Mk.I, a Malta Mk.Vc, a 1943 Mk.IX and a late Griffon-engined Mk.XIV are different shapes with different wings, propellers, exhausts and colour schemes. Decide the aircraft you want to build first, then pick the best kit of that mark.

  • Mk.I / Mk.II — 1940, Dark Earth and Dark Green over Sky or black/white undersides, two-blade or three-blade propeller depending on date.
  • Mk.V — 1941–43, Ocean Grey and Dark Green day-fighter scheme, desert schemes in the Mediterranean, tropical Vokes filter options.
  • Mk.VIII / Mk.IX — the longer-nosed Merlin 60-series Spitfires, the most widely kitted late-war marks.
  • Mk.XIV and later — Griffon engine, five-blade propeller, very different nose.

Recommended Spitfire kits

Best all-round 1/48: Tamiya Spitfire Mk.I

Tamiya’s 1/48 Mk.I is the easy recommendation for a Battle of Britain Spitfire. Engineering and fit are excellent, the parts count is sensible, and it builds into an accurate-looking early Spitfire without fighting you.

Most detailed 1/48: Eduard Spitfire family

Eduard’s 1/48 Spitfire kits (the Mk.IX/VIII/XVI family in particular) are the choice if you want the most detail out of the box, especially in “ProfiPACK” boxings with photo-etch and masks. They have more parts and take more care, so they are better as a second or third Spitfire.

Best value: Airfix newer-tool Spitfires

Airfix’s modern-tool Spitfires in 1/72 and 1/48 are inexpensive, widely available in the UK and much better than the old Airfix moulds. Check the box carefully: Airfix still sells older tools alongside the new ones, and the difference in detail and fit is large.

Best 1/72 detail: Eduard and Airfix

In 1/72, Eduard’s Spitfire Mk.IX family and Airfix’s newer Mk.I and Mk.V kits are the strongest options. Tamiya’s 1/72 Spitfires are also very easy builds.

1/32 showcase: Tamiya

Tamiya’s 1/32 Spitfires (Mk.I, Mk.IXc and Mk.XVI) are among the best large-scale aircraft kits produced. They are expensive and complex, with full engine detail, and reward experienced builders.

Kit ranges change as manufacturers re-box and retire moulds. Always check the release year and reviews of the specific boxing before buying.

Choosing a scale

Which scale?

  • 1/72 — small, cheap and quick. Ideal if you want a collection or have limited display space, but fine detail and painting are harder.
  • 1/48 — the most popular aircraft scale. Big enough for a detailed cockpit and visible weathering, small enough to finish in a few weeks.
  • 1/32 — showcase scale. Superb detail but expensive, large, and every flaw shows.

Painting and markings

Most Spitfire builds go wrong at the painting stage, not the gluing stage. Before buying paint, check the date of your chosen aircraft:

  • 1940: Dark Earth / Dark Green upper surfaces, with Sky, or black and white, undersides depending on the month.
  • From August 1941: Ocean Grey / Dark Green over Medium Sea Grey, with a Sky spinner and fuselage band.
  • Desert and Malta: Dark Earth / Middle Stone over Azure Blue, plus many local variations.

The cockpit is Interior Grey-Green for most marks. Use the paint codes table for brand equivalents and the Spitfire cockpit guide for interior detail.

Quick answers

What is the best Spitfire kit for a beginner?

Tamiya’s 1/48 Spitfire Mk.I or a newer-tool Airfix Spitfire. Both fit well, have a manageable parts count and are easy to find.

What is the most accurate Spitfire kit?

For detail and shape, Eduard’s 1/48 Spitfire family and Tamiya’s 1/32 Spitfires are widely regarded as the best. Tamiya’s 1/48 Mk.I is also very accurate for an early Spitfire.

Which Spitfire mark should I build first?

A 1940 Mk.I is the classic choice: simple camouflage, well-documented squadrons and lots of good kits.

Are old Airfix Spitfire kits worth buying?

Older Airfix tools are cheap but have raised panel lines and poorer fit. Check the box date and look for the newer-tool releases.
