Nakajima B5N Kate

B5N1 / B5N2 · IJN carrier air groups and Kokutai · 1937–1945
Allied desert, Pacific War and Flying Tigers fighter

The Nakajima B5N “Kate” was the Imperial Japanese Navy’s main carrier torpedo and level bomber at the start of the Pacific War. It delivered torpedoes at Pearl Harbor, fought through the Indian Ocean raid, Coral Sea and Midway, and later served from land bases in increasingly worn conditions. For modellers it means a long three-seat canopy, Type 91 torpedo or bomb rack, Hinomaru markings, carrier tail codes, early grey-green finishes, later dark green camouflage and Pacific salt, sun and chipping effects.

B5N operations · 1937–1945

The B5N Kate was the IJN’s key early-war torpedo and level bomber. At Pearl Harbor it carried torpedoes and armour-piercing bombs, while in later carrier battles it attacked ships under increasingly dangerous conditions. A good B5N build starts with the exact role: Pearl Harbor torpedo aircraft, high-level bomber, Akagi/Kaga/Shokaku/Zuikaku carrier route, Midway aircraft, or a later land-based machine with darker camouflage and harsher weathering.

Loadout warning

Pearl Harbor Kates carried different torpedo and high-level bombing fits. Do not fit a Type 91 torpedo to a level-bomber subject without checking references.

Marking warning

Carrier tail codes and Hinomaru styles are date-specific. Avoid mixing Pearl Harbor markings with later dark-green land-based aircraft.

  • Check US star-and-bar / Bomb Group markings, group colours, squadron codes and date
  • Treat early Olive Drab / Neutral Grey/AII, desert, winter, Reich defence and late-war mottling differently
