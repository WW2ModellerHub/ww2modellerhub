Roundel & marking notes

July–Aug 1940: Type A1 roundels (no yellow ring) on fuselage. Type A upper wings. Type B lower wings. Large fin flash.

Sep–Oct 1940: Yellow outer ring on fuselage roundels increasingly common by October.

Markings: Carrier tail codes, vertical fin bands and fuselage bands vary by carrier and date. Pearl Harbor B5N2s often carried carrier identification bands and red Hinomaru without white outlines; check the chosen aircraft before committing to decals.

Cockpit & greenhouse canopy
Modeller's note
The B5N cockpit is long, open-looking and very visible under the greenhouse canopy. Model the pilot, observer/navigator/bombardier and radio-operator/gunner positions as three linked workstations, not a fighter cockpit. Canopy masking is essential.

Engine, cowling & exhaust
Modeller's note
B5N2 aircraft used the Nakajima Sakae radial engine with a black cowling. Keep exhaust and oil staining restrained but visible: carrier aircraft saw salt air, deck handling and warm Pacific operating conditions.

Undercarriage & wheel spats
Modeller's note
The fixed undercarriage and trousered wheel spats define the Kate silhouette. The spats gather grime along the rear edge; tyres should be warm grey rather than dead black.

Torpedo, bomb & racks
Modeller's note
Decide early whether you are building a torpedo aircraft, high-level bomber or later training/anti-submarine subject. Torpedo aircraft need the Type 91 torpedo fit, a clean centreline installation and Pearl Harbor-style operational wear.

Hinomaru, carrier codes & bands
Modeller's note
Carrier identification bands and tail codes are the difference between a generic Kate and a convincing Akagi, Kaga, Soryu, Hiryu, Shokaku or Zuikaku aircraft. Pearl Harbor markings are not automatically the same as Midway or late-war land-based machines.

Airframe, folding wings & surface finish
Modeller's note
The Kate is a large single-engine carrier aircraft with broad wings, long canopy and simple panel rhythm. Weathering should emphasise deck handling, access scuffs, faded upper surfaces, salt exposure and chipped high-traffic areas without turning the aircraft into a derelict.
