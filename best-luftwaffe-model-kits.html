German aircraft kit guide

Best Luftwaffe Model Kits

German WW2 aircraft are some of the best-kitted subjects in the hobby, and their mottled camouflage makes them satisfying to paint. These are the kits to start with.

Fighters

Messerschmitt Bf 109

Eduard’s Bf 109E, F and G families are the detail benchmark; Tamiya’s 1/48 Bf 109G-6 is the easy build. Full comparison: best Bf 109 kit.

Focke-Wulf Fw 190

Eduard’s 1/48 Fw 190A family is very detailed. Tamiya’s 1/48 and 1/72 Fw 190 kits (A, F and D versions) are easier and fit beautifully. The long-nosed Fw 190D-9 “Dora” is a popular late-war subject.

Messerschmitt Me 262

Tamiya’s 1/48 Me 262 is an approachable kit of the first operational jet fighter, with late-war camouflage to match.

Bombers and attack aircraft

Junkers Ju 87 Stuka

Airfix’s modern-tool Ju 87B in 1/72 and 1/48 is excellent and affordable. For the cannon-armed Ju 87G, look at Hasegawa and other specialist releases.

Junkers Ju 88 and Heinkel He 111

ICM’s 1/48 Ju 88 and He 111 families are detailed modern kits. In 1/72, Airfix’s newer He 111 and Revell’s Ju 88 are good options.

Painting Luftwaffe aircraft

Luftwaffe colours follow the RLM numbering system. Early-war aircraft used RLM 70/71 or 02/71 over RLM 65; from 1941 fighters moved to RLM 74/75/76; late-war aircraft used RLM 81, 82 and 83 alongside older stocks. Soft-edged mottling on fighters is the key technique: airbrush at low pressure with well-thinned paint. See RLM paint codes and mottling technique.

Quick answers

What is the easiest Luftwaffe kit to build?

Tamiya’s 1/48 Fw 190 or Bf 109G-6, or Airfix’s new-tool Ju 87B.

What are the main Luftwaffe camouflage colours?

RLM 70/71 and 02/71 over 65 early in the war, RLM 74/75/76 from 1941, and RLM 81/82/83 late in the war.

Do I need an airbrush for Luftwaffe aircraft?

An airbrush makes the soft mottling much easier. Sponge or brush mottling can work but is harder to make convincing.
