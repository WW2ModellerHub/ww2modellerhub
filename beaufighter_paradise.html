Roundel & marking notes

July–Aug 1940: Type A1 roundels (no yellow ring) on fuselage. Type A upper wings. Type B lower wings. Large fin flash.

Sep–Oct 1940: Yellow outer ring on fuselage roundels increasingly common by October.

Code letters: Sky BS381C-216. ~24 inch height. Serial in black rear fuselage port side only. Some Bristol Beaufighters had a Sky spinner with a narrow black spiral stripe.

Stage-by-stage build guide for the Bristol Beaufighter. Assumes 1:48, e.g. Airfix AX05126A.
Period-accurate weathering by base. All Bristol Beaufighters showed exhaust staining port upper cowling. Eight gun port blast marks along each leading edge. Port wing root walkway chipping is distinctive.

Cockpit and observer station
Modeller's noteThe Beaufighter is a crewed aircraft. Treat the pilot cockpit, observer/radar-operator position and access areas as separate modelling zones. Early night fighters need AI radar-operator context; Coastal Command TF.X subjects need navigator/strike-crew logic rather than single-seat ace presentation.

Nose, cannon bay and radar fit
Modeller's noteDo not use one generic Beaufighter nose. Mk.IF night fighters, Mk.VI fighters and TF.X strike aircraft differ in radar aerials, cannon access panels and nose equipment. The nose is the first place a Beaufighter model looks either researched or generic.

Bristol Hercules engines and nacelles
Modeller's noteThe Hercules nacelles are a major weathering feature. Build grime around the collector rings, oil leaks behind cowl panels and long exhaust staining back along the nacelles. Coastal and Far East machines should look harder used than clean RAF fighter subjects.

Undercarriage and stance
Modeller's noteThe Beaufighter sits heavy and purposeful. Pay attention to wheel angle, tyre flattening, nacelle bay shadows and brake-line detail. Far East and Coastal subjects can take more dirt and staining here than a home-based fighter.

Cannon, rockets and torpedo fit
Modeller's noteMatch weapons to theatre and date. Early night fighters are about cannon and radar; Coastal TF.X aircraft may carry rockets, torpedo gear or anti-shipping loads. Do not add rockets just because the kit includes them.

Airframe, panels and surface finish
Modeller's noteThe Beaufighter should look robust rather than elegant. Keep the heavy wing, chunky nacelles and blunt nose visually dominant. Weathering should follow service: damp UK night defence, salt-streaked Coastal Command, dusty SEAC or sun-faded RAAF Pacific.
