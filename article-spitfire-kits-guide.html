Which Spitfire Mk.I kit should you buy? The complete 2025 guide

WW2 Modeller Hub20258 min read

More Spitfire kits have been produced than for any other WW2 aircraft. Choosing the right one matters — the gap between the best and worst is enormous. This guide covers every current injection kit in 1:72, 1:48 and 1:32 that represents a Battle of Britain Mk.I or Mk.II, with honest assessments of accuracy, cockpit detail, fit and value.

Our recommendation

For a BoB Mk.I build in 1:48, the Airfix A05126A (★★★★★) is the definitive starting point. It is accurate, fits well, has excellent panel lines and includes good BoB markings. If your budget stretches, the Eduard 82152 ProfiPACK adds pre-painted colour photo-etch interior, pre-cut canopy masks and outstanding markings — the best out-of-box Spitfire experience available. In 1:72, the retooled Airfix A01071C is streets ahead of the competition at its price point.

1:48 kits compared

Airfix Spitfire Mk.Ia — A05126A
★★★★★
The recommended buy. New tool released 2021. Superb accuracy throughout — correct elliptical wing shape, accurate cockpit dimensions, excellent surface detail. Fits together with minimal filler. Cockpit is good straight from the box; adds significantly with Eduard SPACE or CMK resin. Best value in the scale. Airfix ↗ Airfix ↗
Eduard Spitfire Mk.I ProfiPACK — 82152
★★★★★
Best out-of-box experience. Eduard's own accurate Spitfire moulding with pre-painted colour photo-etch interior (SPACE), pre-cut Kabuki canopy and wheel masks, and exceptional markings covering 92 Sqn, 74 Sqn and 603 Sqn. Slightly more expensive but saves hours of aftermarket purchasing. The cockpit interior is the best in the scale at any price. Scale Model Shop ↗
Eduard Spitfire Mk.Ia Weekend Edition — 84179
★★★★
Same accurate moulding as the ProfiPACK without the extras. Excellent value if you already own Eduard masks or prefer to add your own aftermarket. Good entry point into the Eduard tooling. Kingkit listing ↗
Tamiya Spitfire Mk.I — TA61119
★★★★★
Excellent modern Tamiya tooling with crisp fit and reliable engineering. A strong alternative to Airfix/Eduard for a clean 1:48 Mk.I build. Scale Model Shop ↗

1:72 kits compared

Airfix Spitfire Mk.Ia — A01071C
★★★★★
Best in class. Recent retool matches the quality of the 1:48. Outstanding for the scale — correct wing shape, excellent surface texture, strong BoB markings options (603, 609, 74 Sqn). The obvious choice for 1:72 BoB Spitfire builds. Airfix ↗ Airfix ↗
Tamiya Spitfire Mk.I — TA60748
★★★★
A crisp and easy 1:72 Spitfire Mk.I. The Airfix retool is the better Battle of Britain value choice, but the Tamiya kit is still a strong fallback when available. Scale Model Shop ↗

1:32 kits

Hasegawa Spitfire Mk.I — 08264
★★★
Older mould — surface detail is dated by modern standards. However, the shape is accurate and the large scale rewards superdetailing. Widely available cheaply and a good base for a serious resin-upgraded build.
Revell Spitfire Mk.IIa — 03986
★★★
Note: this is a Mk.II, not a Mk.I. The cowling and exhaust stacks differ from the early BoB Mk.I. Suitable for late-1940 subjects when Mk.IIs were entering service, but check your references. Cheaper than the Hasegawa. Kingkit listing ↗
Avoid
Several older 1:72 Spitfire Mk.I kits from the 1990s (Academy, older Revell, Matchbox reissues) have poor wing shape and oversized cockpits. Unless you're building from nostalgia, skip them in favour of the Airfix or Arma Hobby toolings.
