Dornier Do 17

Do 17E / Do 17M / Do 17Z · Luftwaffe bomber · 1937–1941
Luftwaffe route with Gruppe, Staffel and theatre markings context

The Dornier Do 17, the “Flying Pencil”, was the Luftwaffe’s slim early-war bomber: fast, narrow, heavily glazed and central to the campaigns in Poland, France and the Battle of Britain. It is a different modelling challenge to the He 111: smaller, sharper, more delicate, with a cramped crew gondola, twin fins, long nacelles, splinter camouflage, large bomber codes and the unique RAF Museum Cosford recovery story.

Do 17 operations · 1937–1941

The Dornier Do 17 was designed around speed and a slim fuselage, earning the “Flying Pencil” nickname. It served in Spain, Poland, France, the Low Countries and the Battle of Britain before being overtaken by larger bomber types. For modellers, the Do 17 is about slender proportions, twin-tail alignment, glasshouse nose work, early RLM splinter camouflage, KG codes and careful bomber weathering.

Modeller shortcuts

Build checklist

  • Pick exact Do 17E/M/P/Z variant, KG/recon unit, front, markings and date
  • Check Balkenkreuz / Kampfgeschwader markings, group colours, squadron codes and date
  • Treat early RLM, desert, winter, Reich defence and late-war mottling differently
  • Add Bramo exhaust, cowling oil, gun staining, dust/mud and narrow-gear wear
  • Check Do 17 glazing, guns, bomb bay, camera/recon fit, stores and markings before paint
