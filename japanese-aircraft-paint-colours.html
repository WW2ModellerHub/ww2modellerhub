Pacific colour guide

Japanese WW2 Aircraft Paint Colours

Japan’s Navy (IJN) and Army (IJAAF) had separate paint systems, and manufacturers used their own shades too. These are the principles that get you close.

Imperial Japanese Navy

Early-war Navy aircraft, including the A6M2 Zero at Pearl Harbor, were finished in a light grey with a subtle green or amber tint (often called ameiro or J3 hai-iro). From 1942–43, upper surfaces were painted dark green over grey or natural metal, and the dark green became standard. Engine cowlings were typically a blue-black. Mitsubishi and Nakajima built Zeros used visibly different greens and greys, so check which factory built your aircraft.

Imperial Japanese Army

Army aircraft were often left in natural metal or a light grey-green, then given field-applied green mottling or stripes for camouflage — the classic look of many Ki-43s and Ki-61s. Later aircraft, such as the Ki-84, were factory-painted in an overall olive-brown or green upper surface. Army units used colourful tail markings to identify their sentai (group).

Markings and details

  • Hinomaru red discs, often with a white surround on dark green aircraft.
  • Yellow-orange leading edges on the inner wings as an identification marking, common on both Navy and Army aircraft.
  • Aotake — a translucent blue-green lacquer over metal on interior structure, particularly wheel wells and some cockpit areas.
  • Cockpits in a green (the Mitsubishi and Nakajima shades differ).
  • Propellers usually brown with red warning stripes, or bare metal.

Weathering

Japanese aircraft often show heavy paint loss, especially some Nakajima-built Zeros where the dark green flaked away from the primer. Chip with a sponge or the hairspray method, but base it on photos — not every aircraft was battered. See Pacific weathering.

Paint matches are approximate. Wartime paint varied by batch and weathered quickly, and photos change colour. Use these as a starting point and see the paint disclaimer.

Quick answers

What colour was the Zero at Pearl Harbor?

A light grey with a slight green or amber tint, often called ameiro.

What is aotake?

A translucent blue-green protective lacquer used over bare metal inside some Japanese aircraft.

Why do Japanese aircraft have yellow leading edges?

The yellow-orange inner leading edges were an identification marking.
