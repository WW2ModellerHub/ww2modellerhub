Mosquito operations · 10 July – 31 October 1940

The de Havilland Mosquito served as bomber, night fighter, photo-reconnaissance aircraft, intruder, fighter-bomber and Coastal Command strike aircraft. Its wooden construction, clean lines and high speed make it a superb modelling subject, but the correct variant, radar fit, exhausts, canopy and theatre finish matter. Choosing your Mosquito kit

The Mosquito kit choice depends heavily on variant: B.IV bomber, FB.VI fighter-bomber, NF night fighter or PR aircraft. Check nose, canopy, exhausts, radar aerials, bomb bay and weapon fit before buying aftermarket.

Aftermarket for the Mosquito

Mosquito aftermarket should target the cockpit glazing, canopy masks, exhausts, wheels and variant-specific fittings. Night fighters need radar and flame dampers; Coastal aircraft need rocket rails and cannon detail.

Mosquito paint colours

Mosquito schemes vary by role: day fighter-bombers often use Ocean Grey and Dark Green over Medium Sea Grey, night fighters use Night Black areas, PR aircraft may use PRU Blue and Coastal Command aircraft can carry maritime greys. Match the scheme to unit, variant and date.

Roundel & marking notes

July–Aug 1940: Type A1 roundels (no yellow ring) on fuselage. Type A upper wings. Type B lower wings. Large fin flash.

Sep–Oct 1940: Yellow outer ring on fuselage roundels increasingly common by October.

Code letters: Sky BS381C-216. ~24 inch height. Serial in black rear fuselage port side only. Some Mosquitos had a Sky spinner with a narrow black spiral stripe.

Merlin Engines & Exhaust Staining
Modeller's note Mosquito Merlin nacelles create strong exhaust staining along the sides and wing roots. Night fighters and intruders may need flame-damper exhausts; fighter-bombers and Coastal aircraft often show heavy operational staining around nacelles and access panels.

Undercarriage & Nacelles
Modeller's note The Mosquito sits on substantial main gear inside the nacelles. Check wheel size, tyre flattening, bay colour, door alignment and the stance of the aircraft; nacelle fit and undercarriage alignment are major visual checks on any Mosquito model.

Modeller's note Mosquito markings depend strongly on role and date: bomber, PR, night fighter and Coastal Command aircraft can look very different. Check code letters, roundel type, invasion stripes, PRU Blue finishes, Night undersides and Coastal Command theatre markings before applying decals.

Modeller's note The Mosquito was largely wooden, so avoid excessive bare-metal chipping. Focus instead on paint wear, panel definition, exhaust staining, crew access scuffs, nacelle grime and weathering appropriate to RAF stations, North Sea strike work or night operations.
