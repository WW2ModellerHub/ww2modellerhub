Hurricane kit buyer’s guide

Best Hawker Hurricane Model Kit

The Hurricane shot down more enemy aircraft in the Battle of Britain than any other RAF fighter, yet good kits of it were rare for years. That has changed: there are now excellent options in both main scales.

Recommended Hurricane kits

Best overall: Arma Hobby Hurricane Mk.I

Arma Hobby’s Hurricane Mk.I kits (1/72 and 1/48) are the current benchmark: crisp detail, good shape and sensible engineering. In 1/72 in particular they are among the best small-scale fighter kits available. Arma releases both a basic “Model Kit” and an “Expert Set” with photo-etch and masks.

Best value: Airfix Hurricane Mk.I

Airfix’s modern-tool Hurricane Mk.I kits are easy to find in the UK, inexpensive and build well. They are a good first Hurricane and a sensible choice for a beginner.

Later marks

For the cannon-armed Mk.IIc, tropical Mk.II or the Sea Hurricane, check current releases from Arma Hobby and Airfix first, then older Hasegawa and Italeri tools, which can still build well with some extra work.

Hurricane details to check

The Hurricane changed a lot during 1939–41, and kits often cover several versions with optional parts. Check these before you glue anything:

  • Fabric or metal wings — early Hurricanes had fabric-covered wings; metal wings became standard during 1939–40. See fabric vs metal wings.
  • Propeller — two-blade wooden Watts, de Havilland or Rotol three-blade units, each with a different spinner.
  • Exhausts and kidney plate — the shape and the anti-glare plate vary by production batch.
  • Fuselage fabric — the rear fuselage was always fabric over stringers; a light wash shows the structure well.

Choosing a scale

Which scale?

  • 1/72 — small, cheap and quick. Ideal if you want a collection or have limited display space, but fine detail and painting are harder.
  • 1/48 — the most popular aircraft scale. Big enough for a detailed cockpit and visible weathering, small enough to finish in a few weeks.
  • 1/32 — showcase scale. Superb detail but expensive, large, and every flaw shows.

Painting a Battle of Britain Hurricane

A 1940 Hurricane uses Dark Earth and Dark Green upper surfaces. Undersides were black and white, Sky, or a combination, depending on the month and unit, so check photos of your chosen aircraft. The cockpit is Interior Grey-Green. See Hurricane Battle of Britain colours for the detail.

Quick answers

What is the best Hurricane model kit?

Arma Hobby’s Hurricane Mk.I, in either 1/72 or 1/48, is widely regarded as the best current Hurricane kit.

What is a good first Hurricane kit?

A modern-tool Airfix Hurricane Mk.I: cheap, available and easy to build.

Did Battle of Britain Hurricanes have fabric wings?

Most had metal wings by the summer of 1940, but some fabric-winged aircraft were still in service. Check photos of the exact aircraft.

What colour is a Hurricane cockpit?

RAF Interior Grey-Green, with a black instrument panel.
