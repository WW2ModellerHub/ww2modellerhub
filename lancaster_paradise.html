Choosing your Lancaster kit

For a serious Lancaster build, choose scale first. In 1:48, the Tamiya Lancaster remains the classic large-scale benchmark. In 1:72, the Airfix new-tool family is the best mainstream route and includes standard bomber and Dambusters options. Pick the aircraft subject before buying decals: standard Bomber Command, 617 Sqn Dambusters, Pathfinder, RCAF Mk.X and late-war Tallboy aircraft all need different details.

Aftermarket for the Lancaster

The Lancaster rewards targeted upgrades rather than buying everything. Masks are almost essential because of the greenhouse canopy and turrets. Wheels, exhausts, gun barrels, cockpit photo-etch and bomb-bay details give the biggest visual return.

Lancaster paint colours

Most wartime Lancasters wore Dark Green and Dark Earth upper surfaces with Night Black undersides and lower fuselage sides. Interiors are mainly RAF Interior Green, with black instrument panels, dark turret interiors and heavy operational staining around engines and bomb bay areas.

Roundel, code and marking notes

Standard bomber scheme: Dark Green / Dark Earth upper surfaces over Night Black sides and undersides. The black comes high up the fuselage sides, not just the belly.

Codes: Unit code plus individual aircraft letter. Many mid/late-war Lancasters use dull red or grey-red codes, with some white/yellow theatre-specific variations depending on unit and date. Check your aircraft photo.

Mission tallies and nose art: Bomber Command aircraft often carried bomb symbols below the port cockpit. These were crew-applied and should not look perfectly printed unless using decals.
