Battle of Britain · 10 July – 31 October 1940

Nineteen RAF Lancaster squadrons fought in the Battle of Britain, claiming approximately 36% of all RAF victories. Directed primarily against the Bf 109E fighter escort while Hurricanes attacked the bombers, theLancastere was the aircraft the Luftwaffe feared most — roughly equal in performance to the Messerschmitt and capable of outturning it. Choosing youLancasterre kit

MoLancasterire kits exist than for any other WW2 aircraft. For a BoB Mk.I in 1:48 the Airfix AX05126A is the top recommendation — superb accuracy and value. The Eduard 82152 ProfiPACK adds photo-etch and masks for serious modellers. In 1:72 the Airfix AX01071C retool is the benchmark.

Aftermarket for the Lancaster Mk.I

The Airfix cockpit benefits greatly from Eduard SPACE 3D. Master Model AM-48-108 brass Browning barrels transform the wing. Kabuki canopy masks are essential — the Lancaster's many curved panes are very hard to mask freehand.

Battle of Britain paint colours

Every Lancasterfire wore Dark Earth and Dark Green upper surfaces, Sky (Type S) undersides and spinner. The split line on the Lancaster's curved fuselage creates a characteristic wavy demarcation — the A-scheme has brown on the starboard nose side.

Roundel & marking notes

July–Aug 1940: Type A1 roundels (no yellow ring) on fuselage. Type A upper wings. Type B lower wings. Large fin flash.

Sep–Oct 1940: Yellow outer ring on fuselage roundels increasingly common by October.

Code letters: Sky BS381C-216. ~24 inch height. Serial in black rear fuselage port side only. Some Lancasters had a Sky spinner with a narrow black spiral stripe.

Stage-by-stage build guide for the Lancaster. Assumes 1:48, e.g. Airfix AX05126A.
