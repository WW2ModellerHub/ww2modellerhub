RAF Dark Green Paint Equivalents

Dark Green was on almost every RAF day fighter and bomber from 1937 to 1945. Here is how it was used and which model paints get closest.

Where Dark Green was used

  • 1937–41 Temperate Land Scheme: Dark Green with Dark Earth on the upper surfaces of fighters and bombers.
  • From August 1941 Day Fighter Scheme: Dark Green with Ocean Grey over Medium Sea Grey — the typical Spitfire Mk.V/IX, Typhoon and Mustang scheme.
  • Bomber Command: Dark Green and Dark Earth over black on Lancasters, Halifaxes and Wellingtons.

Choosing a paint

Dark Green was a fairly dark, slightly brownish olive green — not a bright or blue-green. Modern “RAF Dark Green” paints from the main brands are all reasonable matches. Because colours look darker at small sizes, many modellers lighten it slightly (around 10–15% with a light grey or buff) for 1/72 and 1/48, and fade it further on well-used aircraft.

Paint matches are approximate. Wartime paint varied by batch and weathered quickly, and photos change colour. Use these as a starting point and see the paint disclaimer.

Painting tips

  • Spray the lighter colour first (Ocean Grey or Dark Earth), then the Dark Green.
  • Use the official A/B pattern drawings, or photos of your aircraft — patterns were standardised but not identical.
  • Soft but tight demarcations suit most RAF fighters: use putty, Blu-Tack sausages or raised masks.
  • Finish with a satin or matt coat; wartime RAF finishes were matt but weathered to a slight sheen.

Quick answers

What is the Tamiya equivalent of RAF Dark Green?

Tamiya XF-81 Dark Green 2 (RAF).

Is RAF Dark Green the same as Olive Drab?

No. They are different colours, although they can look similar in photos.

Should I lighten RAF Dark Green?

Many modellers lighten it slightly for scale effect, especially in 1/72.
