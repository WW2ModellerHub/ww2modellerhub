Finishing technique

How to Apply a Panel Line Wash

A panel line wash brings out the recessed detail on a kit and gives depth to the finish. It is easy, forgiving and a good first weathering step.

Prepare the surface

A wash must flow into the panel lines without staining the paint around them, so apply a gloss or satin clear coat first. This also helps decals, so many modellers do: paint, gloss, decals, gloss, then wash.

Choose a wash

  • Enamel washes (such as Tamiya Panel Line Accent Color or AK/Ammo enamel washes) flow well and are easy to clean with enamel thinner over acrylic paint.
  • Oil paints thinned heavily with odourless mineral spirit are cheap and very controllable.
  • Acrylic washes are safe over any paint but are harder to clean up.

Rule of thumb: use a wash solvent that won’t attack your paint or clear coat. Enamel or oil over acrylic and lacquer is the safest combination.

Pick the right colour

Pure black is too harsh for most WW2 camouflage. Use dark brown or brown-grey on greens and browns, dark grey on greys and blues, and lighter grey or brown on light undersides. On natural metal, a thin dark grey works well.

Application

  1. Touch a fine brush loaded with wash to a panel line and let capillary action pull it along.
  2. Let it dry for 10–30 minutes (longer for oils).
  3. Wipe off the excess with a cotton bud or cloth lightly dampened with thinner, wiping in the direction of airflow.
  4. Seal with a matt or satin coat.

Don’t outline every line on a small-scale model. Highlight control surfaces, access panels and engine areas, and keep the rest subtle.

Quick answers

Do I need a gloss coat before a wash?

Yes, ideally. Washes stain matt paint and are hard to clean off it.

What colour panel line wash should I use?

Dark brown or brown-grey for camouflage, dark grey for greys and blues. Pure black is usually too stark.

Can I use an enamel wash over acrylic paint?

Yes, once the acrylic and clear coat are fully cured. Use enamel thinner sparingly when cleaning up.
