Cockpit Interior
Modeller's note Hurricane cockpits were painted in Titanine Interior Grey-Green — a complex mixed colour unique to Hawker. The instrument panel was Flat Black with individual instrument details in silver and cream. The floor is typically darker and more worn than the side walls.

Exhaust Stacks & Staining
Modeller's note The Hurricane used ejector exhaust stacks rather than individual stub exhausts. The staining pattern behind them is very consistent — a fan-shaped dark smear across the upper cowling, extending aft to approximately the cockpit. The raw metal of the stacks themselves oxidises to a rusty brown. Eduard Brassin 648580 replicates the stack casting in resin with excellent detail.

Undercarriage
Modeller's note Hurricane undercarriage legs are painted in the interior colour — not in the camouflage colours. The inside of the wheel bays (behind the main gear doors) is also interior grey-green. Exposed mechanical parts on the legs are bare metal. Tyres are a warm mid-grey, not cold grey or black.

Roundels, Codes & Fin Flashes
Modeller's note 1940 BoB roundels changed during the battle. Pre-August 1940: Type A (red/white/blue with yellow outer ring) on upper wing, Type A1 (red/blue no yellow) on fuselage, Type B (red/blue only) on lower wing. After August: some units retained yellow outer ring on fuselage, others removed it. Code letters were 8-inch Night Black (not white) in the standard layout — squadron code, individual letter, squadron code with hyphen. Fin flash was red/white/blue in equal thirds.

Leading Edge Wear
Modeller's note Wing leading edges show the most paint wear. The forward-facing surface suffered from rain erosion, insect impact and maintenance scuffing. The inner leading edge near the wing root is also heavily worn where groundcrew walked during servicing. Use pencil graphite or the hairspray technique here for the best results.

Canopy & Frame
Modeller's note The Hurricane's early canopy has a distinctive boxy, flat-sided greenhouse shape. The framing is painted in the interior colour. The perspex itself is tinted warm amber in period photographs (this is natural aging). The rear-view mirror on the quarterpane is a useful detail to add.

Airframe & Panel Detail
Modeller's note The Hurricane's fabric-covered rear fuselage is a key modelling challenge. The rib structure shows through the doped fabric as subtle raised lines — not as prominent as metal panel lines. The forward metal fuselage has more conventional fasteners. The transition between metal and fabric occurs approximately at the rear of the cockpit.

Cockpit Interior
Modeller's note Spitfire cockpits used a similar Interior Grey-Green to the Hurricane but Supermarine's version was slightly greener. The instrument panel was Black with a red/green dot compass. The spade-grip control column is very distinctive and often poorly reproduced in kits — Eduard Brassin 648578 gives you a superb resin replacement for 1:48.

Exhaust Stacks & Staining
Modeller's note The Spitfire Mk.I used individual stub exhausts rather than the Hurricane's ejector stack arrangement. There are six stubs per side. The staining pattern is multiple narrower plumes rather than one wide fan. This is an important distinction from the Hurricane when applying weathering.
💨 IWM
Spitfire Stub Exhausts — Ground Photography
IWM archive photograph showing the stub exhaust arrangement on a Spitfire Mk.I at dispersal. The individual heat-blackened stubs and multiple staining plumes are clearly visible.
IWM Archive ↗
Undercarriage
Modeller's note The Spitfire's narrow-track undercarriage is one of its most characteristic features. The oleo leg retracts outward into the wing. The leg itself is painted in the same interior colour as the cockpit. The wheel bays are not visible from below on the Spitfire as they are on the Hurricane — the doors close fully.
⚙️ IWM

Period photograph of a Spitfire on grass showing the narrow-track undercarriage in the down and locked position. Note the shimmy damper and oleo compression under aircraft weight.

Leading Edge Wear
Modeller's note The elliptical wing of the Spitfire concentrates leading-edge wear differently to the Hurricane. The wear is most pronounced at the outboard third — the fastest-moving section. The wing root area is less worn. Gun port patches (fabric-covered) are a distinctive BoB detail often missing from models.
✈ Museum

Close photographs of AR501 — the oldest flying Spitfire Mk.Ia — show the elliptical wing leading edge and the typical wear distribution on an aircraft maintained in near-original condition.
Shuttleworth ↗
Canopy & Frame
Modeller's note The early Spitfire used a rounded greenhouse canopy with a brace bar across the top. The framing interior is painted — not bare metal. The cannon fairings on Mk.Ia (8 × .303) have simple rounded gun ports covered with dope patches. The later Mk.Ib had cannon blisters on the upper wing surface.
🪟 IWM

IWM Archive ↗
Airframe & Panel Detail
Modeller's note The Spitfire's all-metal stressed-skin construction means all panel lines are crisp and rivets are prominent. The distinctive "eyelid" cooling slots below the nose are a key identifying feature. The undercarriage doors have a different surface texture to the wing panels and show different wear patterns.
