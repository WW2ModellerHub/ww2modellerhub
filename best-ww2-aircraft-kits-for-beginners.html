Getting started

Best WW2 Aircraft Kits for Beginners

A good first kit fits well, has a sensible parts count and a simple paint scheme. Get those three right and your first build will be a success.

What makes a good first kit

  • Good fit — parts that line up without filler. Tamiya is famous for this.
  • Modest parts count — roughly 40–80 parts for a single-engined fighter.
  • Simple scheme — two upper colours and one underside colour, without complex mottling or natural metal.
  • Single-engined fighter — quicker to finish than a bomber, and the canopy is small.
  • 1/48 or 1/72 — 1/48 is easier to handle; 1/72 is cheaper and quicker.

Recommended first kits

Tamiya 1/48 Spitfire Mk.I

Accurate, excellent fit and a classic RAF scheme.

Tamiya 1/48 F4U Corsair or A6M Zero

Both fit beautifully; the Zero in particular is a very quick build.

Tamiya 1/48 Bf 109G-6 or Fw 190

Easy Luftwaffe options if you want to try mottling.

Airfix newer-tool fighters

Airfix’s modern Spitfire, Hurricane, P-51D and Bf 109E kits are cheap and widely available. Avoid the older tools, which have poorer fit.

Eduard Weekend and Arma Hobby “Model Kit” boxings

Excellent plastic without the extra photo-etch, at a lower price. They have more parts than Tamiya, so they suit a second or third build.

What you need

  • Sprue cutters, a sharp hobby knife and sanding sticks.
  • Liquid cement such as Tamiya Extra Thin.
  • Masking tape for the canopy.
  • Paints for your chosen scheme — check the paint codes table.
  • Decal setting solution to make markings sit down over panel lines.

An airbrush helps but isn’t essential for a first build: many kits can be brush-painted well with thinned acrylics in several thin coats.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Painting the cockpit after closing the fuselage — paint the interior first.
  • Using too much glue, which melts plastic and leaves fingerprints.
  • Applying decals on matt paint — put a gloss coat down first to avoid silvering.
  • Over-weathering. Real aircraft were often fairly clean; start subtle.

Quick answers

What is the easiest WW2 aircraft kit?

Tamiya’s 1/48 fighters (Spitfire Mk.I, Zero, Corsair, Bf 109G-6) are the easiest high-quality kits.

Is 1/72 or 1/48 better for beginners?

1/48 parts are easier to handle and paint; 1/72 kits are cheaper and quicker. Either works.

Do I need an airbrush to start?

No. Thinned acrylics applied with a good brush in several thin coats can give a good finish.
