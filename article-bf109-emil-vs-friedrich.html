Bf 109 Emil vs Friedrich — telling them apart

WW2 Modeller Hub20257 min read

The Bf 109 E (Emil) and F (Friedrich) look similar in photographs, and many modellers — particularly those newer to Luftwaffe subjects — confuse the two. Getting the variant wrong on a finished build is an embarrassing mistake that's entirely preventable. Here are the eight differences that matter.

Why it matters

The Emil and Friedrich were fundamentally different aircraft in terms of performance, armament and operational role. The Emil was the Battle of Britain Bf 109 — the aircraft that fought the Spitfire and Hurricane over southern England in 1940. The Friedrich was its replacement from 1941, a significantly cleaner design that introduced the engine-mounted cannon and removed the underwing guns entirely. Confusing them means producing a historically inaccurate model, and it's visible enough that an experienced modeller will notice immediately.

Difference 01
Nose Profile — the most obvious difference
Emil (E)
Shorter, more blunt nose. The engine cowling is visibly stubby — the distance from the spinner to the front of the windscreen is noticeably shorter. The forward fuselage has a slightly squarer cross-section.
Friedrich (F)
Cleaner, more tapered and elongated nose. The redesigned cowling is longer and more streamlined. The transition from spinner to cowling is smoother with less angular interruption from cooling scoops.
This is the single most reliable identification feature at any angle. The Friedrich's nose reads as noticeably longer and more pointed in profile.
Difference 02
Spinner Shape
Emil (E)
Smaller, more conical spinner with a visible seam line. Often painted in unit colours — white, yellow or black spirals were common on Battle of Britain Emils.
Friedrich (F)
Larger, more rounded spinner with a broader base. The increased diameter of the spinner is visible even in plan view and becomes the dominant feature of the Friedrich nose profile.
Difference 03
Oil Cooler — the most debated detail
Emil (E)
Asymmetric oil cooler under the port wing — a large, rectangular intake protruding below the wing leading edge. This is absent from the starboard wing. One of the most distinctive Emil features in plan or three-quarter view.
Friedrich (F)
The underwing oil cooler is completely removed and relocated into an enlarged chin-mounted annular cooler integrated into the lower cowling. The wing undersurface is clean with no protruding cooler intake.
This alone is conclusive identification. If you can see the underwing oil cooler, it's an Emil. If the wing underside is clean and there's a chin intake on the cowling, it's a Friedrich.
Difference 04
Wing Cannon Blisters
Emil (E-3, E-4, E-7)
The E-3 and later sub-variants carry 20mm MG FF cannon in the wings. The cannon breach creates two distinctive blisters on both the upper and lower wing surface, visible as rounded bumps near the leading edge.
Friedrich (F)
No wing cannon — the Friedrich removed the underwing armament entirely. The wing surface is completely clean with no blisters. Armament moved to the engine-mounted MG 151 cannon firing through the spinner.
Note that the E-1 and E-1b sub-variants also have clean wings — only the E-3, E-4 and E-7 have the cannon blisters. Check your sub-variant.
Difference 05
Exhaust Stacks
Emil (E)
Six individual, round exhaust stacks per side — twelve total. They protrude clearly from the upper cowling and are individually distinct when viewed from the side. Often a target for aftermarket brass replacements.
Friedrich (F)
Six ejector-type exhausts per side with a slightly different profile and slightly more forward-swept angle. The ejector design produces a characteristic rearward thrust that was intended to add a small amount of thrust.
The difference is subtle but visible in close-up — the Emil exhausts are rounder and more uniform; the Friedrich exhausts have the characteristic downward-swept ejector profile.
Difference 06
Horizontal Stabiliser — rounded vs squared
Emil (E)
Horizontal stabiliser has a rounded, more organic tip profile. The tail unit appears slightly less angular in plan view. Bracing strut visible between stabiliser and fuselage on all variants.
Friedrich (F)
Completely redesigned horizontal tail with a more angular, semi-rounded tip. The stabiliser is symmetrically profiled and the strut is removed — the cleaner tail silhouette is clearly visible even in small-scale photographs.
Difference 07
Undercarriage Legs
Emil (E)
Narrow-track undercarriage common to all Bf 109 variants. The Emil's legs are visible as a relatively straight profile without the distinctive fairing shape of later variants.
Friedrich (F)
Revised undercarriage with a slightly different fairing profile. The main strut has a more pronounced forward angle and the gear door shape differs slightly from the Emil.
Undercarriage differences are minor and not reliable for identification at a distance — prioritise nose, spinner and oil cooler first.
Difference 08
Canopy / Windscreen
Emil (E)
Flat-sided, angular canopy with prominent framing. The windscreen has a distinctive three-panel flat-glass design with a curved armour glass panel in the centre. The overall canopy outline is boxier and taller.
Friedrich (F)
Redesigned canopy with improved pilot visibility. More streamlined windscreen profile. The framing is simplified. The overall canopy line is lower and cleaner than the Emil. Many late Friedrichs also had a modified headrest fairing.
Quick-check guide
If you're looking at a photograph and need to identify the variant quickly: first check the wing underside. Underwing oil cooler blister = Emil. Clean wing underside = Friedrich. Then check the nose — stubby = Emil, elongated and clean = Friedrich. Those two checks alone will correctly identify 95% of all Bf 109 E and F photographs.

Sub-variants at a glance

Within each mark there are further sub-variants that matter for accuracy. The Emil family runs from E-1 (no wing cannon, October 1938) through E-4 (main BoB variant, two wing cannon) to the E-7 (drop tank equipped, late 1940). The Friedrich family runs from the early F-1 and F-2 (20mm MG FF/M engine cannon) to the F-4 (upgraded 15mm or 20mm MG 151) which was the primary Friedrich variant from mid-1941 onwards.

For Battle of Britain subjects, the E-4 is the correct kit choice — it represents the primary production variant in service from August 1940. For North African or early Eastern Front subjects, the E-7 (with tropical filter option) or F-4 are the appropriate choices.
