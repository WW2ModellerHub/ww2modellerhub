Martin B-26 Marauder

B-26B / C / F / G · USAAF · 1942–1945
USAAF / USN route with unit, theatre and markings context

The Martin B-26 Marauder was a fast, compact, high-wing-loading medium bomber with a controversial early reputation and an outstanding later combat record. It became a key USAAF tactical bomber over North Africa, Italy, Normandy, France and Germany. For modellers it means short-wing versus long-wing choices, Olive Drab or natural metal, invasion stripes, flak damage, mission markings, turret detail, tight nacelles and the famous “Flak-Bait” route.

B-26 operations · 1942–1945

The B-26 Marauder was not as famous as the B-26 or B-24, but it became one of the most effective American medium bombers in Europe. It operated with the Ninth Air Force, Twelfth Air Force and tactical bomber groups, attacking bridges, rail yards, airfields and troop concentrations. Modelling it well means choosing the exact production block and theatre: early short-wing aircraft, MTO Olive Drab machines, Normandy invasion-striped aircraft, late natural-metal examples, or Flak-Bait.

Variant warning

B-26 short-wing and long-wing aircraft differ. Check wing, tail, turret and nose details before buying decals or aftermarket.

Finish warning

B-26 weathering is often mission-worn rather than wrecked: faded OD, exhaust, oil, flak patches, invasion stripes and dusty airfields.

Modeller shortcuts

Build checklist

  • Pick exact B-26A/B/night fighter variant, Bomb Group/USAAF Bomb Group/recon unit, front, markings and date
  • Check US star-and-bar / Bomb Group markings, group colours, squadron codes and date
  • Treat early Olive Drab / Neutral Grey/AII, desert, winter, Reich defence and late-war mottling differently
  • Add Bramo exhaust, nacelle oil, gun staining, dust/mud and narrow-gear wear
  • Check B-26 glazing, guns, fighter stores, gun bay, camera/recon fit, armoured attackes, stores and markings before paint
