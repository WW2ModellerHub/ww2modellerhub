Choosing your Zero kit
Do not choose a Zero kit by “A6M” alone. Early A6M2 Model 21 carrier aircraft, A6M3 Model 32 clipped-wing subjects and A6M5 late-war aircraft have different wings, exhaust/cowling details, colours and marking logic.

Zero build guide
1 · Choose variant first

A6M2 Model 21, A6M3 Model 32/22 and A6M5 all need different wing, exhaust and marking choices.

2 · Decide theatre/date

Pearl Harbor, Midway, Rabaul and 1945 home defence should not share the same paint and wear pattern.

3 · Cockpit before canopy

The framed canopy exposes the cockpit. Add belts, panel contrast and careful canopy masking early.

4 · Paint markings where possible

Hinomaru and tail bands look better when painted into the same faded/chipped surface as the camouflage.

5 · Keep the airframe light

Do not over-rivet. The Zero’s appeal is clean, lightweight lines interrupted by localised operational wear.

6 · Finish to theatre

Carrier aircraft need salt, deck handling and exhaust staining; Rabaul aircraft need dust, humidity, fading and rough-field grime.

Zero weathering guide
Zero weathering is not one universal “chipped Japanese aircraft” recipe. Early carrier aircraft can be relatively clean and salt-worn; late island-based aircraft can be sun-faded, dusty, patchy and heavily handled.
Carrier A6M2
Pearl Harbor / Midway
FinishWarm grey-green with subtle deck grime.
WearWing root scuffs, cowling staining, restrained chipping.
Model tipKeep contrast subtle; do not make every Pearl Harbor aircraft a late-war wreck.
Rabaul / Solomons
1942–43
FinishHeat, humidity and dust over field-maintained paint.
WearLeading edges, access panels, cockpit sill and fuel/oil staining.
Model tipUse layered fading and selective chipping rather than random silver speckling.
Late-war A6M5
1944–45
FinishDark green upper surfaces often look tired and chalked.
WearHeavy cowling staining, dull hinomaru, chipped service panels.
Model tipCombine faded green panels with a slightly satin blue-black cowling.
Special attack aircraft
Philippines / 1945
FinishRushed maintenance and hard-used aircraft.
WearPatchy paint, worn tyres, stains around fuel and cowling panels.
Model tipTreat the subject respectfully and accurately; avoid cartoonish damage.
